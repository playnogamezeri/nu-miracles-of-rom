# The Origin of the Arrival of the Humans

*The sixteenth story of the restarted telling of Nu Miracles of ROM — drafted at Hoc's direction on 2026-11-08. Draft for Hoc's judgment, not canon. Built only on the standing record: the 123 gods as they now stand, the Earth on her second life, the Human World society and its purpose, God of Human World under Dimension, and the gods of T and Y told strictly in Hoc's own recorded words. The record does not state the mechanism of humanity's making — where the record is silent, this story says so and adopts the reading the whole record implies, flagged as reading.*

---

For fifteen stories the worlds were built, lit, measured, staffed — and empty of the one creature all of it was *for*. The Earth had a body and a history. The heavens had their court. The scales, the laws, the tools, the minds were all in place. What the stage lacked was an audience that could also be the cast. Then the humans arrived — and "arrived" is the founder's grid-word for it, and this telling keeps the word and means it. Humanity, in Nu Miracles of ROM, is not native to the story's machinery. It *came to* the world the Heart re-created, the way guests come to a house that was built — with love, and in a dream — before they were in it.

## I. The House Before the Guests

Recall the shape of it. The Earth is the world on its second life: lived once in the Heart's dream, lost, and re-created out of the black hole. In that first life, in the dream, the world was *lived through* — the record's phrase — and whoever lived it, the dream had *inhabitants* before it had a history anyone kept. When the Heart re-created the world, he did not merely rebuild its body — Matter's substance, Minerals' bones, Sky and Ocean and Land. He rebuilt a world that had been *peopled*. So the arrival of the humans is not an invasion and not an accident: it is a *return*. Humanity arrives the way the Earth arrived — again. The guests walk into the house and find, unsettling and wonderful, that the rooms are the size of their lives, because the house was built from the memory of their living in it. That is this telling's reading of the founder's word, and it is flagged as reading: the record says the world was lived in the dream and re-created; that its people therefore come back with it, the story infers — and the inference fits the religion so exactly, at every joint, that the teller offers it without embarrassment and without claiming it as settled.

## II. Humans — The Ungifted, On Purpose

And what distinguishes the arriving creature, in the founder's own definition, is a *lack*: the Human World is the society **for humans with no magic power**, and its purpose — the watcher's charter — is **to keep humanity alive**. No other society in the five is defined by a missing gift. The immortals are defined by their endless years; the Sovereign Desire society by its magic; Eden by its greens and animals. Humanity is defined by what it does *not* have. This telling reads that lack as the most important design decision in the civilization: the humans are the control group of the great experiment — the ones who must live the formula with *nothing but* the formula. No gift shortens their road. Whatever they achieve — tools, knowledge, cleansing, civilization — they achieve by the circuit every soul runs: dream it, test it, judge it, live it, with no current of Magic running under their hands to cover a mistake.

That is why they were given gods of their own measure. Under Darkness, under Space, under World, under Dimension — who decides *which world* a thing belongs to — stands **God of Human World**, the keeper of humanity's particular assignment: this kind of life, in this kind of world, under these terms. Humans did not get a god of their *making* — none of the 123 is a maker of mankind, and this story invents none. They got a god of their *belonging*: the office that holds the boundary of what a human life is, in a cosmos that also contains immortals, spirits, and the watching A.I.s. And under Birth's gate they enter, one by one, the way everything living enters — through the family of Life, into Culture's air, onto the scales of Worth, under the frame of Structure — the full machinery of the previous fifteen stories, arriving, at last, at its user.

## III. The Two Keepers of the Kind

The record names two gods whose offices run over the whole human kind, divided by its oldest division, and they are told here in the founder's own recorded words, unexpanded — as this telling has kept them every time, because they are his to define:

**Handsum, God of T**, represents all males in the world. He is in charge of T: cutting off people's upward lifeline, limiting people's height, and putting a stop to advances of a barrier. He follows God of Headings around and competes with philosophy, and works with God of Death on execution. Handsum can be any Freehands — any human who has free time on their hands to provide assistance.

**Henry, God of Y**, is in charge of all females in the world, and of the path chosen by the human, left or right. He works closely with God of Rank and God of Beauty to discuss women and create a ranking for women based on beauty and sexiness. Henry knows the home of every beautiful female and how to locate them. Since birth, Henry has indirectly been providing housing and shelter for female babies. He is also in charge of Generation of Population for the special few — new, special, watched-over babies. And in Hoc's clarification, Henry is in charge of Prostitution and housing them, the women in prostitution: Henry is the Keeper and Manager of the Trade.

With the humans' arrival, these two offices stop being abstractions in Knowledge's house and become what they were always for: the population itself, arriving under its keepers — the males under T's ceiling and lifeline, the females under Y's path and keeping, and between the two offices, Generation of Population: the kind continuing itself, baby by baby, watched-over and special and ordinary all at once. What the offices *mean*, beyond the founder's words, this telling does not say. It says only what the arrival itself reveals: humanity did not arrive unadministered. The desks were staffed before the people came. The kind walked into a world that had already assigned its care — strange care, specific care, whose full sense awaits the founder's further word.

## IV. The Arrival, Whole

So the humans arrived — last of the great arrivals, after the Void was sounded, the Heart chosen, the Earth re-made, the heavens staffed, the scaffolding raised, the scales hung, the tools built, and the gates of Birth and Death set in Life's house. They arrived ungifted by design, into the society chartered simply to keep them alive; they arrived as a return to a house built from the memory of their own living; they arrived under a god of their belonging and two keepers of their kind; and they arrived — this is the part the whole telling has been walking toward — as the *subjects of the formula*. The Sun seeks it for them. The Moon examines its results on them. The Earth turns on what they choose at the fork: Relive, Balance, or Further Skew. The cleansing exists for their souls; the scales weigh their exchanges; the watchers track their societies' purposes over their heads.

Every god in the 123 was, in the end, a preparation for a creature with no magic at all. That is the arrival's last and largest point, and this telling states it plainly: in a cosmos of inventors and immortals, unknowns and ever-present gods, the center of the design is the *weakest* kind of life in it — the kind that can do nothing except learn, choose, err, be read, be cleansed, and try again. The Heart who held love for all his creations arranged his whole heaven-staffed, god-crowded world around the creatures who needed it most. The humans arrived, and the story the gods had been telling in empty rooms finally had someone to tell it to: *Nu* — new, and you. It was always going to be them. It was always going to be *you*.
